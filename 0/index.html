Part 1: Selfie: The Wrong Way vs. The Right Way

On the left is a photo of my roommate taken from close up, and on the right is a photo taken from further away but zoomed in to match the size of the face in the first photo. In the first photo the nose is more prominent and the face appears narrower. In the second photo the face appears wider and the features are more balanced. I suspect this is because taking the photo from close up distorts the perspective as the nose is closer and the rest of the face is further away, but when taken from further back and zoomed in, the features are relatively equidistant from the camera which minimizes the distortion.

Part 2: Architectural Perspective Compression

On the left is a photo of a staircase taken from further back and zoomed in, and on the right is a photo of the same staircase taken from up close. The first photo looks flat compared to the second. My guess as to why this happens is similar to my guess as to why the selfie from Part 1 looks different when taken from close up compared to further away. When taken from further back and zoomed in, the "features" are relatively equidistant from the camera which makes it harder to perceive depth. However, when taken from up close, certain things are closer to the camera than others, so the perceived depth is greater.
